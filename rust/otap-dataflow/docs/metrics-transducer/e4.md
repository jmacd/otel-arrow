# E4. Durable identity for sealed OTAP publications

[Worklist](../metrics-transducer-worklist.md) | Depends on A2, C1-C2 and D2-D4.

A regional stage may receive the same edge result on a different gRPC stream,
in different Arrow batches, after either process restarts. On an opt-in link
it must count the result once. Start with sealed publications only; ordinary
OTAP streams retain their existing contract.

## Logical publication and coverage

A cell is one rule output series over one aligned `(start, end]`. Persist
the logical writer, generation, rule, cell and revision as a publication
key at the edge's output commit. A sealed-only cell has one revision. Carry
its input coverage, seal and representation with it. These are protocol
metadata, not resource or point attributes used to group series. Preserve
writer identity until reconciliation; two edges contributing the same
service are not duplicates. Negotiate one representation per contribution;
reject a second feed of the same coverage in another temporality rather
than add both the delta and the cumulative view from which it was derived.

Each child writer also declares completion for its rule windows, even
when no cell was produced. For example, a durable seal manifest can name
the expected publications for a window; an empty manifest explicitly proves
no contribution. Verify and durably accept every listed value before
declaring coverage. A gap-free ordered stream through a seal can provide
equivalent evidence. Silence, a later timestamp and the largest seal yet
seen do not distinguish an empty minute from a lost batch. Do not create a
zero-valued series to encode emptiness.

The identity invariant is firm; **wire placement is open**. The [OTAP
exporter][exporter] correlates `BatchStatus` by stream-local `batch_id`.
Neither that ID nor static stream headers identify a cell. [Pdata context]
could carry metadata on links that preserve publication boundaries.
Otherwise retain a per-point association (e.g., columns or a keyed manifest)
across every split and merge, or disallow that batching on the capable path.

## Durable acceptance and bounded retry

Validate a key and digest of its logical content, independent of Arrow
layout. Atomically record acceptance with the recoverable input or regional
effect and coverage state before ACK. The [receiver][receiver] needs a
downstream result from this barrier; queue admission or `wait_for_result`
alone is not durability. A matching retry is acknowledged without another
effect. Conflicting content for a retained key gets a permanent NACK and a
counted diagnostic. A batch succeeds only when all its publications qualify.
The sender advances its durable reader cursor only over acknowledged work.

Bound outstanding keys, bytes, writer generations and retry age. Persist
a rejection floor before pruning digests; never treat an older, now unknown
key as fresh. Reject and count expired retries, exposing a gap requiring a
new agreed cut or explicit resynchronization. Fence retired generations.
After losing its checkpoint a writer must use a new generation and agree
on non-overlapping coverage, not replay old intervals under fresh IDs.

## Acceptance example

Edge A commits `service=api, +7` and a seal for the next, empty minute.
The region durably accepts both; the ACK is lost. After reconnect, resend
them separately and in reverse order. A recovered region still has 7 and
proof of the empty minute. If B publishes its own +7 the result becomes
14. Change A's retained +7 to +8 and the result stays 14 with a conflict;
retry below the negotiated floor and the result stays 14 with a stale error.
