# A2. Evaluation boundaries, lateness and finality

[Worklist](../metrics-transducer-worklist.md) | [Refinement protocol](e5.md)

A scrape is a read, not a clock tick. For each rule group, persist the event
time through which the evaluator has sealed results and the input cut used
to make that decision. Replaying a quiet hour tomorrow must reproduce the
original decision, including any declaration of missing input.

## Time and progress

- **Grid:** for origin `O` and period `P > 0`, a pane ending at `T = O + n*P`
  covers `(T-P, T]`. A point exactly at `T` belongs to the ending pane.
  Evaluation cadence, query range and the cumulative output's start time are
  separate. Start with nested edge and regional grids on the same UTC origin.
- **Cut:** a durable input position, or a vector of positions for independent
  writers, through which all inputs and control decisions are accounted for.
  Receiving a high timestamp is not evidence that an earlier item arrived.
- **Seal:** a monotonic decision for a boundary and cut. A complete seal
  means all required source progress is accounted for. A configured cutoff
  may instead seal an explicitly incomplete result. A provisional publication
  does not advance this boundary.

The evaluator owns progress. Source progress or an explicit timer-driven
cutoff may trigger evaluation. Idle sources must be able to report progress
without inventing a metric sample. For an untimestamped UDP source, the cut
describes accepted datagrams, not everything a sender might have sent.
Record the decision, participating writers, missing coverage and cut with the
[commit](d2.md); replay the decision rather than reading the current clock.
An expired reader, disconnected child or missing revision cannot be silently
classified as an empty interval.

## Interval and late-arrival policies

A timestamped event can be placed at its event time. An aggregate with known
coverage contributes its full value when its interval fits the required pane.
For the first exact evaluator, reject a contribution that straddles a required
boundary and report incomplete coverage. Keep cumulative observations for
later differences. Do not duplicate, prorate or invent the missing boundary
sample. For example, an increase of 12 over `(10:00:30, 10:01:30]` does not
determine the increases in the two adjacent one-minute panes.

Before sealing, admissible late input may change open cells. The baseline
sealed-only policy rejects and counts input for a sealed pane. With negotiated
refinement, a bounded restatement horizon permits a newer revision; it does
not rewrite the record of what was committed at the original cut. Outside the
horizon, report the rejection and preserve history. Bound open windows even
when no writer advances ([C5](c5.md)).

## Acceptance

Give two children a five-minute parent window. One child reports through
10:05; the other's last complete position covers 10:04. The parent stays
open until the missing progress arrives or the recorded cutoff marks it
incomplete. Restart on either side of that decision. The same cut must yield
the same value, boundary and completeness, including when a client scrapes
between the two child reports.
